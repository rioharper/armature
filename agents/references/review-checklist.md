# Review Checklist — Gap Taxonomy

The families where robotics designs fail review, in working order: the early ones are cheap to check and expensive to miss. Interfaces (4) and cross-document consistency (7) earn more attention than they seem to deserve; they are where projects quietly die.

## 1. Requirements

- Is every requirement **quantified** (number + unit) and **verifiable** (a stated test / analysis / inspection / demo)? "Fast", "robust", "user-friendly" are aspirations.
- Do any requirements **contradict** each other — mass budget vs. stiffness, top speed vs. runtime, precision vs. cost?
- Does any single requirement **contradict itself**? A sweep that reads requirements against each other misses the one whose own clauses cannot both hold — usually a term of art whose definition forbids the clause beside it ("open source, non-commercial": the OSI definition forbids field-of-use restrictions, so nothing satisfies both). Quantified and verifiable is not satisfiable, and a Must written this way is unbuildable rather than tight.
- What is **missing**? The ones forgotten until they bite: thermal limits, duty cycle, EMI/noise, ingress (dust/water), maintenance access, storage and transport, regulatory/safety, end-of-travel and e-stop behavior.
- Do the success criteria capture the **actual mission**, or an easy-to-measure proxy for it?

## 2. Traceability

- Does every **Must** requirement map to a design element that satisfies it *and* a test that proves it? In `docs/01-spec/traceability.md`, a Must REQ with no analysis or test row is a hole.
- Any **orphan design** — subsystems or features that trace to no requirement? Each is a missing requirement or gold-plating.
- Do the numbers **survive the chain** mission → requirement → feasibility calc → chosen part → BOM? Each hop preserves the number or explains why it changed.
- **Routed items**: a decision report's *Items routed here* table is the exit ledger for everything sent to that task. Read it against the sources that did the routing — the plan leaf's issue body, the Route lines of earlier reports in `docs/reviews/`, the TBDs of the milestone notes — and name every item with no row. An item with no row is an item lost, and it was routed because someone judged it load-bearing.

## 3. Physics, math, and budgets

**Five error families produce derivations whose arithmetic is right and whose model is wrong.** Every printed number reproduces, every algebraic step holds, every self-consistency check passes — because the mistake sits on *both sides* of each of them. They are named here because a finding with a family name routes itself; each points at the probe below that hunts it and at the one file that owns its rule. They add no family to this taxonomy: all five live inside this one.

| Family | Probe below | The file that owns the rule |
|---|---|---|
| A **frozen degree of freedom** — a coordinate the derivation fixes that the machine leaves free | *An assumption that freezes a coordinate, with no release behind it* | **armature-derive** `references/derivation-standards.md`, *The assumption table*; the skill's Step 0 |
| A **worst case at named configurations** — "worst", "any", "anywhere" answered by a handful of postures | *A claim evidenced at named configurations*; *A worst case taken at named configurations, or by a handful of samples* | same file, *Evidence over an envelope*; the skill's Milestone 3 |
| A **load-case sign read one way** — a signed acceleration or pull sized in its gentler sense | *A signed load case read in one sign* | same section, and `params.py`'s `LOAD_CASES` table with `params_checks.py` enforcing it |
| A **formula outside its assumptions** — a book's closed form on a mechanism outside its class | *A closed form cited without its applicability check* | same file, *A closed form from a book* |
| **Unswept singular values** — a matrix inverted whose rank was assumed | *An inverted matrix with no singular-value sweep*; *A mode named without a linearisation behind it* | same file, *Evidence over an envelope*; the skill's Milestone 1 |

All five were found on the first real project through this plugin, in one review, after its checkpoint had passed.

- **Re-run the feasibility math** with the artifact's own numbers. Does it close at the edge of the envelope, or only at nominal?
- **Margins**: is each sized to its stakes? Actuator torque, structural stress, thermal, power, current. State the margin the consequence warrants and compare to what is there.
- **Budgets** (`docs/01-spec/budgets.md`): current estimates against each budget, and whether recent changes were debited at all.
- **Reserves**: a reserve row holds a Must requirement, so its margin is a pass/fail, not a reading — negative means the reserve is breached and that REQ has failed. Recompute it: ceiling minus every other line's current estimate, against what the REQ demands. A breach the prose above the table admits while the row's margin and `docs/01-spec/traceability.md` still read healthy is a Blocker; so is a debit that grew a line item and left the reserve untouched.
- **Price staleness**: a cost row is only as fresh as the `Retrieved` date of the `docs/datasheets/index.md` row it cites — that date is the price's date, and the only thing that says it has gone off. Check it against today's. Over six months with nothing re-read is a Minor; a cost margin that closes only on a price over a year old, or that is thinner than the drift its category has shown (a Jetson dev kit's list price rose 60 % in nineteen months), is a Major; a purchase authorised on one is a Blocker. Route the fix to the `armature-librarian` agent — re-reading a vendor page needs the web, which you do not have.
- **Assumptions vs. envelope**: for every assumption the derivation names (rigid links, neglected friction, lumped rotor inertia), does the real operating range cross the line where it breaks? A report's *Assumptions under strain* owes each one the **direction and size** its model gives — swept, with what the result reads at each end, and the task that closes it named. One listed without that is one nobody swept.
- **An assumption that freezes a coordinate, with no release behind it**: the platform stays level, the base is rigid, the elbow is locked, the gearbox has no wind-up, the cable is inextensible. Each removes a coordinate from the equations, so it is missing from **both sides** of every check made with them — the arithmetic reproduces, the self-consistency holds, and the machine does something else. Read `00_setup.md`'s assumption table **Released?** column (`references/derivation-standards.md`, *The assumption table*): each such row carries either the name of the check that released it in the truth model and what moved, with units and sign, or one sentence on why the coordinate is not in the parameter block and what adding it would take. Re-run the release with Bash. A blank cell is a Major; a released value the note reports and then designs past is a Question; an assumption freezing a coordinate that a released check shows moving past the margin is a **Blocker**, and it is the finding that blocked the first real project through this plugin — a platform assumed level that gravity tilts 5° to 26°.
- **Dynamics checked against nothing but itself**: M symmetric positive-definite, Ṁ − 2C skew-symmetric, energy conserved under integration — every one of those holds M, C and g against each other or against a property *any* correct Lagrangian has, so a term missing from T or V is missing from both sides of all of them. A milestone that claims a torque and carries no **independent inverse-dynamics check** — the truth model's τ against the derivation's M q̈ + C q̇ + g, at sampled states with nonzero velocity, not at rest — has no evidence its dynamics describe this machine, only that they are consistent. Re-run it with Bash; a note whose only dynamics evidence is self-consistency is a Major, and one that sizes an actuator from it is a Blocker.
- **A mode named without a linearisation behind it**: a mode frequency is an eigenvalue of (K, M), and an entry of K is the stiffness one coordinate feels moving alone — the two are the same number only when both matrices are diagonal. A note that quotes a mode taken from a matrix diagonal, from a closed form whose assumptions it never checked, or from a stiffness matrix whose **rank it never computed**, is quoting a number about a motion the mechanism may not have (a stiffness matrix that is rank 3 has three modes, whatever its diagonal has entries for). Ask what was linearised, at which configuration, with what step; re-run it with Bash. No linearisation is a Major, and a mode sizing a controller bandwidth or a structural member is a Blocker.
- **A claim evidenced at named configurations**: home, q = 0, fully outstretched, a workspace corner, the symmetric load case. Every one of those is a symmetry plane, and a symmetry plane is where a wrong assumption cancels — the moment that would tilt the platform is zero there, the rank a structure matrix loses there is the rank it has everywhere. Evidence for a claim over a set is a **seeded random sample of that set with its size and seed stated** (`references/derivation-standards.md`, *Evidence over an envelope*); named configurations are the report module's, for the reader. Re-run the check with Bash at your own seed. A claim whose configurations are all named is a Major; one where the named postures are the mechanism's symmetry planes and the requirement is about "anywhere" is a Blocker. The first project through this plugin passed at every named posture and failed at the reviewer's first random one.
- **An inverted matrix with no singular-value sweep**: a Jacobian pseudo-inverted for IK, a structure matrix solved for tensions, a stiffness matrix inverted for compliance. Ask for σ_min swept over the sampled envelope, printed by the report module, and for the bound the derivation asserts on it and **over which set** — the envelope is what the joints reach and `WORKSPACE` is what the requirement names, so a bound asserted on the wrong one is a bound relaxed to whatever the joints allow. σ_min is a rate: its reciprocal is the joint speed and joint force a unit of Cartesian motion costs at the worst posture, so "it looked fine" is not a reading. No sweep is a Major; a rank claimed and never computed, or a singularity inside the intended workspace, is a Blocker.
- **A worst case taken at named configurations, or by a handful of samples**: "worst", "any direction", "anywhere in the workspace" name a *set*, and a few postures are not an answer about a set. Ask which method answered it — an exact set method where the set is convex (an LP, a hull, a zonotope, a norm with a closed form), a multi-start optimiser where none exists, or a dense sweep **with its grid step printed beside the result**. One start is one basin; a grid alone is blind to a peak narrower than its step, so the two run together and a gap between them is the finding. Re-run the search with Bash at a finer grid and more starts. A worst case from named postures or from random samples is a Major; one that sizes an actuator, a structural member or a reserve is a Blocker. On the plugin's own example the optimiser beats the 25-point grid at every joint.
- **A signed load case read in one sign**: a deceleration, a base acceleration, a cable pull, a wind load, a thermal excursion. Check `params.py`'s load-case table has a row per `(class, magnitude, direction set, sign)` and **both signs of every signed class** — the sign is a row that exists or does not, never a detail of whoever read the requirement. A 0.5 g vertical case read as upward when downward is the one that binds the floor is exactly the finding on the first project through this plugin. One sign in the table is a Major; a part sized from the gentler sign is a Blocker. A load whose requirement names a *set* of directions and whose row names one direction is the same finding in a different clothes.
- **A closed form cited without its applicability check, or an algorithm without its chapter**: a formula out of a book has somebody else's arithmetic, so it reproduces, and the only open question is whether *this* mechanism is in the class the formula is a theorem about. Ask which truth check compared it against the truth model at **sampled** configurations — not at the posture where the cosines are all one, which is where a formula outside its class most often happens to be right — and which assumption the check names. A form with no such check is a Major; one sizing a part is a **Blocker**: the first project through this plugin took a bifilar yaw stiffness on cables that converge rather than hang parallel, and was 27× low. Separately, a method taken whole from a book — a workspace method, a force-distribution law, a stiffness model — is tagged `standard` and cited by book and chapter in `00_setup.md`, because reading the derivation against the book is the job and it needs the book named. A method cited without its chapter is memory, and a Minor; one whose book assumes a geometry this machine does not have is a Blocker.
- **Singularities & workspace**: does any singularity sit *inside* the region the robot must work in?
- **Units and signs**: check them explicitly.
- **Printed, not typed**: every table in a derivation note or report is printed by a report module under `analysis/model/` (`report_*.py`). Run it and match the tables against what it prints; a number with no module printing it was typed by hand, and a typed number goes stale in silence when the model moves under it.
- **Datasheet reality**: are the numbers driving the design confirmed from datasheets (`docs/datasheets/index.md` rows), or assumed? An assumed number inside a load-bearing calculation is a Question at best and a Blocker at worst. A row's **Clauses read** cell bounds what was verified — probe what it leaves open, and take as answered what it covers. A row marked **CANDIDATE, pending user confirmation** is unconfirmed: a design resting on one is a Blocker.
- **Book data**: a constant tagged `standard` in `params.py` — fatigue endurance points, finite-life tables, friction pairs, form factors — has no index row to cite, so it names its book, edition, and the table or figure it sits in, and carries a **second independent source** with the design sized to the conservative of the two. A book named without its table is memory. One source is a margin resting on one fit: shot-peened spring-wire endurance points differ by 30 % between two standard sources that agree within 6 % unpeened.

## 4. Interfaces

For every seam between subsystems, check it is defined **on both sides** and that the two sides **agree**:

- **Mechanical** — mounting patterns, tolerances, load paths, clearance, service access. Can it be assembled, with a tool on every fastener afterward?
- **Electrical** — voltage, continuous *and* peak current, connector pinouts, grounding. Does the driver's rating cover the motor's worst-case draw?
- **Data** — protocol, rate, latency, timing, and the units and coordinate frames carried across the boundary. A frame convention that flips between two documents is a shipping bug. Read these off the spec's §8 link table and name what is missing: a link with no budget, a budget with no verification method, a data artifact crossing units with no named schema owner, or a compute unit on a host no requirement forces it onto.
- **Thermal** — where does the heat go? Is there a path, or does a component cook in a sealed bay?

## 5. Failure modes and safety (FMEA-lite)

- For each major part: what happens **when** it fails? Graceful, or catastrophic?
- Where are the **single points of failure**, and is each acceptable given the stakes?
- Behavior on **power loss, comms loss, e-stop, and out-of-range command** — defined, or undefined?
- **Safety coverage**: the spec's mechanical-safety section, item by item, against the actual design.
- Is there a **maintenance and repair path**, or does one worn bearing mean a full teardown?

## 6. Scope vs. capability

- Does the scope still match the **builder's honest capability, weekly hours, and deadline** (the spec's capability assessment states these)?
- Is there **iteration reserve**? A plan with no slack to revise the worst mechanism ships the first guess.

## 7. Cross-document consistency

Run last; it needs every artifact in view at once.

- Do the **derivation's parameters match the spec's** — link lengths, masses, payload, gravity?
- Does the **plan's BOM match the spec's chosen parts**, or has one been revised without the other?
- Do **frames, symbols, and naming agree** across spec, derivation, and plan? The derivation consumes the spec's symbol table verbatim.
- Are the documents at **revs that reference each other correctly**, or is the derivation validating a spec two revisions old?
