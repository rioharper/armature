# Changelog

## Unreleased

Five changes: the plan moves off disk onto GitHub Issues, subagents stop inheriting the main session's model, the derivation's machinery becomes one engine compiled from a single mechanism description, every worst case is searched once, by that engine, and actuators and the bus are sized from a simulated mission. The first two are recorded as ADRs — [0001](docs/adr/0001-plan-as-a-parallel-issue-species.md) on why the plan is its own issue species rather than a wayfinder map, [0002](docs/adr/0002-github-is-a-hard-prerequisite.md) on why the local tracker was deleted.

### Mission sizing

Actuators and the bus are sized from a simulated mission ([#83](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/83)): one engine module, `armature.drive`, holds the drive data with every constant's basis, the missions, the shared DC bus and the margins ([ADR 0006](docs/adr/0006-actuators-and-the-bus-are-sized-from-a-mission.md)), and spec §9 runs those margins on a coarse description, which derive refines in place ([ADR 0007](docs/adr/0007-actuator-and-bus-feasibility-runs-from-spec.md)). A margin with missing data is UNCHECKED and fails. The sizing calculator, the static holding torque as the sizing case, "motion profile TBD" and §9's hand arithmetic for actuators are gone.

- **Drive data and the electrical model, every constant with its basis** ([#84](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/84)). `armature.drive` gains `Motor`, `Gearbox`, `Driver` and `Thermal`. Kt, Ke, resistance and every current name their basis as required keywords (`Kt(1.22, per="A_rms", side="output")`, `Ke(9.6, per="V_rms/krpm", between="line", side="motor")`, `Resistance(0.55, between="line")`, the motor's `wiring`), and `kt_from_kv(kv, convention="sinusoidal" | "trapezoidal")` makes 8.27/Kv against 9.55/Kv a choice. `check(motor, n)` fails a Kt more than 2 % from Ke in Lee et al.'s power-invariant d-q convention: Ibex's RobStride 02 agrees to 0.86 % at N = 7.75, and Plumb's mj5208 at 9.55/Kv fails by 15 %. It also fails an envelope that misses a published `Curve` by more than the curve's `agree`. `electrical(tau, omega, n, motor, gearbox, driver, temperature, rotor)` takes one actuator's output samples to rotor torque, signed by the load's τ·ω (τ/(N·η) motoring, τ·η_b/N back-driven), plus the rotor's own J_m·ω̇_m, which no efficiency touches, and to d-q current, voltage, R_φ(T), copper loss and bus power. `envelope(motor, n, v_bus)` gives no-load speed k_mod·V/Ke, with k_mod √(1/2) for wye and √(3/2) for delta, and stall at the lower current limit. `heat` integrates the winding's single-node RC exactly, with R(T) feeding back, and `repeat` runs a trace once per period until a cycle starts within 0.1 K of the periodic state or the session ends. The spec's "under 0.1 K per cycle" read 0.95 K short of a 3.47 K rise where the time constant is ten periods. `rms_torque` is the fallback where no RC is published.
- **Missions: moves, holds and a followed q(t), sampled through `inverse` to each actuator's motor torque and speed** ([#85](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/85)). `armature.drive.Mission(name, segments, duty=Duty(period, session))` runs `move(start, end=…)` or `move(start, direction=…, distance=…)`, rest to rest under `Limits(v, a, j, along="coordinate" | "path")`, a required choice, since a 2 m/s limit on each of x, y and z is 3.46 m/s on the diagonal, and phase-synchronised by ruckig so it travels a straight line in the independent coordinates; `hold(posture, duration, loads=…)`; and `follow(q, duration)`, the escape hatch, whose rates are differenced where not given. `trace(m, mission, rate)`, at `SEARCH_RATE` (200 Hz) or `CONFIRM_RATE` (1 kHz), calls `m.at(q, qd)` then `inverse(qdd, loads)` at each sample and returns time, q, q̇, q̈ and each actuator's motor-side torque (rotor N²J_m included, gearbox lossless) and speed (N times its joint's rate), with the rotor's share J_m·N·q̈ as `rotor`; `Trace.output(actuator)` gives the load across the gear, N·(τ − rotor) at ω/N, as `electrical` takes it. A mission's `actuation(state, qdd, loads)` replaces `inverse` for an allocation the engine's least squares does not make, such as a cable robot's positive tensions. A move's `start`, `direction` and `distance` take a `Coordinates` state, a `Direction` vector and an `Interval` value as they come. A geared pendulum's swing reads its closed-form peak and RMS motor torque to 2e-16; a five-bar's torques are `inverse`'s at every sample. ruckig (`~= 0.19.4`) joins the engine's runtime dependencies.
- **The shared DC bus: its source, capacitance, brake resistor and flux brakes** ([#86](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/86)). `armature.drive.Drive(motors=…, drivers=…, gearboxes=…, bus=Bus(actuators, source=Source(v=(lo, hi), sinks=…, p_max=…, overvoltage=…), capacitance=(lo, hi), brake=BrakeResistor(threshold, r, energy, power), hotel=…))` is a project's `drive(p)`, with `gear` and `rotor_inertia` read from the compiled model. `charge(trace, drive, capacitance=…, v_source=…, v0=…)` runs the bus from `v0` (by default V_s) at one point in each band, as #88's `Interval(*band)` axes will read it. It takes each actuator's `Trace.output` through `electrical` to bus power and integrates C·dV/dt as the capacitor's energy, exactly within each substep. Each sink is a clamp that holds its level while its capacity lasts and lets V past when it runs out: the source at V_s, sourcing up to `p_max` and sinking only if `sinks`, so a PSU behind a diode never takes current back; the brake as an ideal chopper, V²/R above its threshold; and each driver's flux brake, which absorbs up to i_d²·R_φ above its onset in the power-invariant convention, the spec's 1.5·I_d,peak²·R_φ. The substep halves until halving it again moves the peak by less than `agree`. `Charged` gives V(t), its peak and minimum, the brake's energy over the trace and in its largest single pulse and its peak power, the source's peak power, its uncapped `source_demand` (so an undersized source fails a margin rather than only sagging), its peak sink and its energy, and each flux brake's heat on the trace's own times, which is `heat`'s `power=` input for that motor. The no-sink pulse reads √(V₀² + 2E/C) to 1e-12. The geared swing at N = 50 on 220 µF reaches 92 V, twice a 44 V trip, matching the energy relation on its largest run of returned energy to 1e-4. The probe's 30 V, 8 Ω brake holds it at 30.0 V, where the probe's 30.2 V matches a forward-Euler chopper's overshoot at its 0.05 ms substep. An overloaded brake's refined peak lands within 2·`agree` of scipy's ODE.
- **Events: coast, short and flux-brake, forward-simulated** ([#87](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/87)). `armature.drive.event(start, modes, duration, loads=…)` is a mission segment simulated rather than prescribed. It runs from a `State`, from a posture at rest, or from `after(segment, fraction)`, where the segment just before it stops at that fraction, rates and all, so an e-stop partway through a move inherits its speed. `modes` maps every actuator to `"coast"`, `"short"`, `"flux_brake"` or a torque profile, each a d-q current against the back-EMF E. A short is i = −E/R_φ, a joint damping of N²·Kt·Ke/(R_φ·η_b). A coasting motor carries no current until E passes k_mod·V, where its bridge's diodes regenerate into the bus. A flux-braking driver brakes at the short's current up to its limit, and past that limit regenerates; its flux brake then holds the bus at the onset. The simulation runs on a copy of the compiled model under `implicitfast`; the engine's own model keeps Euler. Contact goes through the compiled pairs, and the bus is stepped with the mechanics from wherever the segments before it left it. `trace(m, mission, rate, drive=…, capacitance=…, v_source=…)` samples each event at every timestep and reruns it at half that step. `Trace.events` reports both runs, each with its bus voltage, its contacts and how far halving moved it. `Trace.driven` tells `charge` where a coasted or shorted driver's flux brake is off. A shorted flywheel reads exp(−c·t/J) to 1e-4; a coasting pendulum keeps its energy to first order in the step; a flux-braking flywheel reaches its 30 V onset in the ½C(V² − V₀²)/P the regen gives and holds it within `agree`; fixture 3, held by torque profiles, rests on its wheel with the weight it carries to 1e-3.
- **Drive margins, searched** ([#88](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/88)). `armature.drive.margins(drive, missions, over=space)` returns one ADR 0005 `Margin` per check. Per actuator, the checks are: every (τ, ω) sample inside the envelope at the bus's minimum voltage, with the winding hot; driver peak current; winding temperature over the duty, or RMS torque against the motor's continuous rating where it publishes no `Thermal`; and driver RMS current where the driver is rated for it. Per bus, they are: peak V against the lowest trip; minimum V against the undervoltage trip; the brake resistor's pulse energy and continuous power; and the source's power. `drive` is a `Drive`, a project's `drive(p)` (its tolerances as `Band(name, BANDS, of="drive")`, which the space neither checks against the mechanism nor compiles), or a mapping of bus configurations, enumerated as `"drive"`. `missions` mixes missions as written with searched ones, `mission(point) → Mission`, enumerated as `"mission"`. The peak margins search every axis, including move endpoints from `Coordinates`, `Direction` and `Interval` axes, event trigger fractions, and the bus's capacitance and source-voltage bands as their own axes. The duty margins run only the missions as written, over their duty, searching only bands. The margins of one space share each point's evaluation. The bus is charged with the windings at T₀ for peak V and the brake, which returns the most regeneration, and at each `Thermal.t_max` for minimum V, the source's power, flux heat and the envelope's V_min, which draws the most. Each margin's `truth` re-runs its point at 1 kHz with every event at half its timestep, and must agree within 1 % of its limit. A margin with missing inputs is `Margin(unchecked=...)`: its `Worst` reads UNCHECKED, names what is missing, and fails. `energy(...)` reports each written mission's energy, and `review(margins)` writes each worst case to `review/` as a PNG whose `Panels` text names its plots; matplotlib joins the development dependencies. On the 2R arm, the elbow's worst move is the shoulder's, 0.34 A_peak below the elbow-only move, and the search matches a 360-move sweep. Traced at 5 Hz, the elbow move fails its 1 kHz truth. A swing's peak V is worst at the least capacitance and the most efficient gearbox. **Fixed in `trace`:** an event's boundaries are now sampled on both sides at one time, so the bus takes the torque's jump as a step. It had smeared the jump over a sample interval, and an e-stop's peak V converged only at first order in the rate (0.7 V off at 200 Hz on 100 µF; 0.09 V now).

### The engine: one mechanism description, compiled through MuJoCo

A project now writes its mechanism down once, as `mechanism(p)` in `analysis/model/mechanism.py`: bodies, joints, sites, cables, loops, contacts and actuators, with every number in the base frame at one home posture. Called with floats, the engine compiles it into the MuJoCo truth model; called with SymPy symbols, it gives the derivation each body's frame and points, so a frame is written once and cannot drift. Each assumption that removes a coordinate becomes a named reduction (`lock`, `couple`, `planar`, `lump` or `rigid`) carrying its assumption number, and the reductions make the variant a milestone reads. Releasing one sizes it by name, and `armature.account` fails a derivation whose coordinates are not its variant's. The queries read floating bodies by name and answer loops and contact with explicit constrained solves, where `truth/` misread or refused them, and stiffness and modes now count springs and cables. The machinery is one package, `engine/`, imported as `armature`, which a project pins rather than copies.

This replaces the DH table, the `FLOATING_*` dicts and the `truth/` package that *The derivation gets a truth model* below adds. That section's rules stand; its machinery is gone. [#64](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/64) tracks the effort. [ADR 0003](docs/adr/0003-one-mechanism-description-at-the-home-posture.md) records why the description is written once, at the home posture, and [ADR 0004](docs/adr/0004-model-machinery-is-a-pinned-package.md) why the engine is a pinned package and not a copied template.

- **`engine/` is a new package, imported as `armature`** ([#65](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/65)), pinned to `mujoco ~= 3.15.0` and tested by `uv run pytest` in its own environment. A description is `mechanism(p)`, built from `body`, `joint`, `site` and `cable` (plus an optional `gravity`), with every number in the base frame at home. `armature.compile(mechanism, params)` validates it before MjSpec is touched (duplicate names, unreachable bodies, non-unit axes, unphysical inertia and undefined parameters, each naming its element), compiles it in radians with explicit inertia, and `m.at(q=..., qd=...)` gives an immutable state keyed by coordinate name. `s.linearise()` reads joint springs and cables, which `truth/modes.py` reads as 0 N/m: on fixture 5, a 0.5 kg mass on a 1000 N/m spring or cable reads 1000 N/m and 7.1176 Hz, and the slack cable reads 0. `m.model` is the raw `MjModel`, kept reachable for code that needs its arrays.
- **Fixture 1, the 2R arm, runs end to end through the description** ([#66](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/66)).
  - **Actuators.** `actuator` puts a motor on a joint, with a gear ratio, a rotor inertia compiled as N²J armature, and a motor-side torque limit.
  - **Queries.** A state answers `pose`, `jacobian`, `inverse`, `settle` and `wrench`, each as a named record carrying its residual and method. Loads are forces at named sites, applied where they act.
    - `inverse` is an explicit least-squares solve for the actuator torques. It returns the rank, the conditioning and the residual, and the residual is what the actuators cannot supply.
    - `settle` quenches for at least 500 steps.
    - `wrench(body)` is the wrench across the body's joint, about its anchor.
  - **The symbolic view.** `armature.symbolic(mechanism, params)` gives a SymPy frame and points per body, plus axes, masses, inertia dyadics and gravity.
  - **Variants.** `reduce(mechanism, lock(...))` names one. A lock compiles as a weld with no joint equality, and `s.release(hold)` reports each locked coordinate's displacement by name.
  - **Fixture 1 checks.** FK, the Jacobian, M, g, the inverse dynamics and the elbow's reaction wrench match the closed forms at sampled states, and the inverse agrees with `mj_inverse`. The locked-elbow trap fails its release, as it must.
  - **Bug fixed.** `linearise` read M after `mjd_inverseFD` had left it at a perturbed posture, so M was evaluated at q − eps. On the 2R arm that is off by 4e-7.
- **Fixture 2, a platform on four cables, runs through the description** ([#67](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/67)).
  - **Floating bodies.** `joint(name, "free", parent="world", child=..., point=...)` makes a floating body. Its value is a `Placement`, the point's position plus a 3×3 rotation, so no quaternion component is ever read as an angle. Rates and every matrix follow `m.dofs`: `name:x` to `name:z` for the point, then `name:rx` to `name:rz` for the angular velocity, all along base axes. Positions are written through `jnt_qposadr` and rates through `jnt_dofadr`, and a hinge child on a floating body reads its own angle.
  - **Locks on a floating body.** `lock("hang:rx", ...)` holds one component. The free joint is rebuilt from the scalar joints that remain, so a level platform is three slides. Release reports each locked turn by name.
  - **Records.** `settle` reports each cable's `tension`, exactly zero when slack. `wrench` counts cable forces, so a hanging platform's free joint carries nothing, while a level platform's slides carry the moment its welded turns hold.
  - **Fixture 2 checks.** The four-pose settle table of the 2026-09-15 research note reproduces: tilts of 0°, 20.8°, 14.7° and 26.3°, with the slack cables at the low corner. The settles match an independent minimisation of the potential. The level-platform assumption holds at the room centre and fails, as an expected failure, at the other three poses. The 6×6 stiffness matches the potential's Hessian, and yaw matches m g A d / z0.
  - **Breaking.** `Jacobian`, `Linearised` and `Inverse.generalised` are keyed by dof, not coordinate, and `qd` and `qdd` take dof names. For a mechanism with no floating body the names are unchanged.
- **Closed chains: loops and couples, solved explicitly** ([#68](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/68)).
  - **Elements.** `loop(name, a, b)` closes two sites that coincide at home. It compiles as a site-based `connect`, never a body-anchored one, which would hold an assembly error in place. `couple(a, b, ratio, assumption=...)` is a reduction tying one coordinate to another, as a belt does, and compiles as a joint equality. The compiler sets both constraints' solver parameters. At impedance 0.9999 a five-bar at rest holds 3e-9 m of slack; MuJoCo's defaults left a parallelogram creeping for 20 s.
  - **Assembly.** `m.independent` names what `at()` takes, and `m.dependent` what it solves: unactuated joints first, a couple's follower first. `at()` closes every loop to 1e-10 m along the branch from home, predicting along the branch's tangent so a nearby crossing branch is not taken. It solves the dependent rates so the loops stay closed. A posture that cannot close raises, naming the loop. A posture that closes with a joint outside its range also raises.
  - **Inverse dynamics** solves [S J_cᵀ][τ; λ] with each loop's and couple's rows built from MuJoCo's Jacobians. It never calls `mj_inverse`, and a test pins that. It returns each loop's force as a 3-vector and each couple's as a scalar, with `rank` out of `unknowns`. A planar loop keeps only its two in-plane rows, so a lost rank means the same on a planar loop as on a spatial one. An unknown the solve cannot determine reads NaN, never a least-norm number.
  - **Modes** on a closed chain are on the constraint manifold only. They come from central differences of the reduced force in the independent dofs, re-closing the loops at each step. `wrench` counts each loop's force from the same solve.
  - **Settle** reports how far open the loops are and raises past 1e-6 m.
  - **Fixture checks.**
    - On a planar five-bar, the static torques match virtual work to 3e-12, whatever the solver settings, while `mj_inverse` is off by more than 10 %. K and the modes match a closed-form minimal-coordinate reference to 3e-10, and no solver-stiffness mode appears.
    - On fixture 4, a balanced parallelogram arm, gravity torque is zero over the envelope, and a counterweight 0.5 kg light reads its closed form. At the singularity the solve reports rank 3 of 4 and a NaN loop force; a milliradian away the same force reads 10⁴ N.
- **Coarse bodies, the last two validation checks, and the Milestone 0 review artifact** ([#71](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/71)).
  - **Coarse bodies.** `point`, `rod`, `box`, `cylinder` and `sphere` each return a body whose mass, COM and inertia in base axes at home come from its dimensions and a mass or a density; `compose` joins several into one by the parallel-axis theorem. They are plain arithmetic, so the symbolic view reads exact closed forms. A point, or a rod alone, has no inertia about itself and fails validation, as MuJoCo would.
  - **Validation.** A loop whose sites are written more than 1e-6 m apart at home fails, naming the loop and the gap. A parameter that is defined but never read fails, naming the parameter. **Breaking:** a params dict that carries values the description does not read is now refused, on the truth side and the symbolic side alike.
  - **The review artifact.** `armature.frames(mechanism, params)` is the frames table: every body's mass, COM and inertia, every joint's axis, point, range, home and spring, every site, cable end and loop gap, and the actuators, gravity, parameters and a variant's reductions, in the base frame at home, with `.markdown()`. `armature.render(state, path)` draws a state's skeleton offscreen as PNG: inertia boxes, joint axes, each body's COM, sites, cables, any contacts, and the base frame. `armature.review(mechanism, params, directory, states)` writes `frames.md`, `home.png` and a PNG per named state. On fixtures 1 and 2 the table matches MuJoCo's own world-frame kinematics at home. Pillow, which writes the PNG, joins the development dependencies.
- **The rest of the reductions, sized by name** ([#70](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/70)).
  - **`planar(plane)`** locks, at home, every coordinate that would leave the plane. An Ibex-shaped torso on two five-bar legs under `planar("xz")` keeps x, z and pitch and its in-plane joints; a neck yaw and a cross slide lock.
  - **`lump(body, into)`** moves a body's mass and inertia into a neighbour by the parallel-axis theorem (total mass and the home COM unchanged), onto a site as a point mass, or, `into=massless`, away. MuJoCo refuses a moving body without mass, so the body left behind compiles with 1e-6 of its own, and `m.approximate` says so; a test pins the refusal. The symbolic view is exact.
  - **`rigid(element)`** holds a cable at its rest length as a constraint row, so `at()` solves it and `inverse` reports its tension, or locks a sprung joint where its spring rests. `rigid` on a contact arrived with the contact element (#69).
  - **Release** is keyed by reduction in `Released.sized`. A lock, couple or planar is sized by displacement, as before; a couple by its follower's departure from the coupled value. A lump or a rigid is sized by `quantity=`, as its change between the variant with and without that one reduction: a rigid cable on fixture 5 releases with the elastic cable's sag, m g / k, to 4e-12.
  - **`armature.account(m, coordinates)`** fails a derivation whose coordinates differ by name from its variant's, naming each coordinate: one dropped without a reduction, or one a reduction holds. A coordinate a closure solves may be left out.
- **Contact: wheels and feet, solved explicitly** ([#69](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/69)).
  - **The element.** `contact(name, body=..., shape=shape(...), against=shape(...), mu=...)` sets a body's sphere, cylinder or box against a fixed plane, sphere, cylinder or box, with Coulomb friction μ. It compiles as an explicit pair between two geoms that collide with nothing else. Without a compliance it is rigid, at the loops' solver parameters, and a settle that leaves it sunk in past 1e-6 m raises. With `stiffness` (and optionally `damping`) a sphere is a physical tyre: the compiler scales MuJoCo's direct solref by its own inverse-mass estimate, so the tyre sinks by its load over k. `rigid(contact)` drops the compliance.
  - **Friction that rests.** The cone is pyramidal and the noslip solver runs wherever friction acts. In MuJoCo 3.15 noslip changes nothing on an elliptic cone. The settle's rest test now reads the speed each step reaches before the quench zeroes it. Read after the quench, a steady creep with no acceleration had passed for rest. Without noslip, a compliant tyre on a 10° incline creeps and never rests.
  - **Inverse dynamics** adds each touching contact's rows at MuJoCo's contact points, in the directions its joints let it move. Rows that coincide, such as a planar wheel's two rims, merge into one. `Inverse.contacts` reports each contact's normal force, friction force and friction-cone margin μN − |F_t|, from the same solve as the torques; `mj_inverse` is never called. A load two contacts share in a way nothing decides reads NaN. `wrench` counts the contact forces, and `Settled.contacts` reads MuJoCo's own forces at rest.
  - **Modes with contact** come from `mjd_transitionFD`, run twice from rest with the rigid contacts and loops at solref 0.005 and 0.02. A root that moves between the two runs is the solver's own. It is discarded and listed with both of its values. Each eigenvalue is inverted through the Euler step exactly: ln λ / dt would read a 17.9 Hz mode 5e-4 high.
  - **Checks.** A wheel on a 10° incline comes to rest with normal and friction forces of M g cos α and M g sin α to 1e-4 N; the explicit solve gives them, and the hub torque, to 1e-9. Its linearisation keeps the 0.96 Hz rocking mode and discards three solver roots. A hub spring's 17.9 Hz servo mode is kept, matches its closed form to 1e-4, and moves only with its own stiffness. On fixture 3, a five-bar leg on a wheel, the three motors' static torques match virtual work to 1e-8, the wheel carries the weight less any lift, and the cone margin's sign follows μ times the load. The frames table gains a Contacts section.
- **The seeded sampler moves into the engine** ([#72](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/72)), from the template's `truth/envelope.py`, which stays until #73 rewires the template.
  - **Independent coordinates, loops closed.** `armature.sample(m, n, seed)` draws each independent hinge and slide uniformly over its joint range, or over a narrower `within` box such as the workspace, and `at()` closes every loop. A draw that cannot close is never kept. It is counted in `Samples.rejected` under what refused it: the loop, or the joint that closing would put out of range. `at()` now raises that as a `ClosureError` naming it.
  - **Floating poses.** A floating body is drawn from a `pose_envelope(box=, tilt=, yaw=)`. Its point is uniform in the box, its up axis is uniform over the cap within the tilt of gravity's up, and its yaw (the twist about up once the lean is taken out) lies within its bounds. A free joint has no range, so a floating body without an envelope is refused. A variant's rebuilt chain draws its slides from the box and its turns within ±tilt or the yaw bounds, and keeps only poses inside the envelope, so a planar torso's pitch is a tilt.
  - **Contacts.** Contact takes no part in `at()`, so `rest_on={"tyre": "y"}` solves one coordinate per contact until that contact just touches. `Samples.clearance`, `reached(contact)` and `armature.clearance(state, contact)` report how far apart a contact's shapes are, from `mj_geomDistance`.
  - **Rates.** `sample_states` adds rates and accelerations uniform within ± the limits it is given, both signs, keyed by independent dof. They come from a second generator, so its postures are `sample`'s, and the dependent rates are solved so the loops stay closed. `rest_on` is refused there, because a resting contact's rates are not solved.
  - **Reporting.** Every result prints n, the seed, the draws refused by reason, and each contact's reach.
  - **Assembly** walks from home only along the joints around a loop, couple or rigid cable, and sets the rest at their targets at once. A wheel turned 90 rad used to cost 1,800 continuation steps and now costs none.
  - **Fixture 3's trap.** A skid pad round the knee reaches the floor only where the leg swings. Swept over every leg length at α = 0, from 7 to 29 cm, it stays at least 5 cm clear. 24 samples with the tyre resting on the floor find it touching at four, all at |α| ≥ 0.64 rad.

### The worst-case search: one search over a named space, for every margin

Ten of the 36 Blocker and Major red-team findings in Plumb and Ibex read a worst case at one named posture, direction, sign or nominal value, when the true worst lay elsewhere, and every search in sight was a grid, a factorial or a bisection that shared no code. A project now declares each claim as a margin, slack against its derated limit over a space of named axes (posture, load case and sign, load direction, tolerance band), and the engine's `armature.search` finds its worst: a seeded Sobol set, every corner, then a Powell polish. The template discovers every margin, searches it, confirms its worst in the truth model, and fails it below zero; the derivation standards' three-tier method rule and the corner sweep as a separate run are gone. [#75](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/75) tracks the effort, and [ADR 0005](docs/adr/0005-worst-cases-are-searched-once.md) records why a worst case is searched once, by one engine module, with its method fixed.

- **A query no longer allocates its own `MjData`** ([#76](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/76)). Each compiled model keeps one scratch `MjData`, which every query, and `at()` on a closed chain, resets and fills from its state. A query run while another holds it, because one called the other, gets its own. Every answer matches a fresh compile's to the bit. On the 2R arm, `jacobian` falls from about 1 ms to 30 µs and `inverse` from 1.2 ms to 100 µs; what is left of `inverse` is the NumPy of its solve, not the physics. A compiled model and its states are for one thread at a time.
- **The sampler's map, shared with the search** ([#77](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/77)). `armature.sampler.unit_cube(m, poses=, within=, rest_on=)` maps a point in [0, 1]^d to a state, or to a `Rejected` naming the loop, joint, contact or pose envelope that refused it. The same point always gives the same state, and corner 1 is each range's end. `axes` gives the order of a point's values and `d` their number. A floating body spans six axes: x, y, z, the share of the tilt cap, the lean's azimuth and the yaw. A coordinate `rest_on` solves is no axis. A rebuilt chain's components span the box and the tilt and yaw bounds, and a point whose pose falls outside the envelope is a rejection, never a redraw. `sample` and `sample_states` feed the map from their seed, and a test pins their output bit for bit against what they drew before. The one change is that a rejected draw no longer carries the unread value it drew for a `rest_on` coordinate. The sampler stays for the truth checks; Overhaul 02's search drives the same map.
- **The worst-case search, on plain callables** ([#78](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/78)).
  - **Margins.** `Margin(name, fn, unit, over=space)` is slack against a derated limit: slack ≥ 0 passes, and −∞ is legal. `Space(**axes)` names its axes, `Interval(lo, hi)`, `Choice(values)` and `Direction("xy" | "xz" | "yz" | "sphere")`, and `fn` reads a `Point` by axis name. A point outside the space raises `Infeasible(reason)`; a design failing there is a number.
  - **The method is fixed.** Each `Choice` combination gets a scrambled, seeded Sobol set (64 per continuous dimension, rounded up to a power of two), every corner, a direction's being its axis directions in both signs, and bounded Powell from the best three. `Budget` sizes it, and nothing else tunes it. The result is the least slack over every point evaluated, since at a kink Powell ends worse than the corner it started on.
  - **The record.** `Worst` gives the value and where, whether it passes, the worst per combination, n, the seed, the pre-polish best and where, the rejections by reason, and the fraction of the global set that passes. `str()` is the row a note cites. A `nan` raises, naming the point.
  - **Goldens.** The 2R arm's holding margin over q1 ∈ ±π, q2 ∈ ±2.6, a 20 N tip load in any direction in the plane and m2 ± 10 % reads its closed form, 0.1713052 N·m, to 2e-14 in 971 evaluations: the worst posture is interior and the worst mass a corner. The cable platform's tension floor over x ∈ [1.5, 2.5], y ∈ [1.6, 2.2], z ∈ [0.9, 1.4] reads 4.987626 N at the mirror corner (2.5, 1.6, 0.9), across the slope jump at x = 2.2.
  - **The polish may step 0.05 past the unit cube**, and is evaluated at the nearest face there. Bounded at the cube itself, Powell stalled once a coordinate reached a face, and stopped 1e-4 short on the arm after 2,000 evaluations.
- **Band and Coordinates, the axes that touch the mechanism** ([#79](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/79)).
  - **`Band(param, bands)`** spans a parameter's tolerance band, read from a mapping the template passes as `params.BANDS`; the engine reads no project file. In a space with `Coordinates`, each band's value overrides its parameter before the state compiles, 1–3 ms a point. A band that names no parameter of the mechanism, or whose nominal lies outside it, raises when the space is made, before anything compiles.
  - **`Coordinates(mechanism, params, within=, poses=)`** gives `fn` a ready state, `p.state`, from the sampler's map: loops closed, floating bodies inside their envelope. Its corners are its ranges' corners, and a point the map refuses is `Infeasible` under the map's reason. The mechanism compiles at nominal when the space is made, which fixes the axes, and a band that would change them raises; without a band it never compiles again. `at` writes the state as its independent coordinates.
  - **Goldens through the axes.** The 2R arm's holding margin, with m2 as a band and the posture as `Coordinates`, reads 0.1713052 N·m to 1e-16 at m2's upper end, in 976 evaluations. The cable platform's tension floor, over the level variant's three slides across a pose envelope's box, reads 4.987626 N at (2.5, 1.6, 0.9).
  - **The five-bar over ±π.** Three of its four corners and about 30 % of the square cannot close. The pin's clearance above a floor 0.30 m down reads its closed form, both legs straight, to 1e-16 in 594 evaluations, and lies below a dense run of the same margin refined round its best; the refusals are counted under `loop 'pin'`, `joint 'B'` and `joint 'D'`.
- **The model template declares margins; verification and σ_min move onto the search** ([#80](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/80)). **Breaking:** `verification.py`'s `grid_search`, `optimiser_search`, `worst_case_torque` and `worst_over_load_cases` are removed, with `GRID_POINTS`, `OPT_STARTS`, `case_torque`, `combine` and its `Worst` namedtuple; so are `kinematics_checks.py`'s 400-draw σ_min check and `SWEEP_SAMPLES`. A project that calls them declares a `Margin` instead.
  - **Discovery.** `run_all.py` finds every module-level `Margin` in a milestone module or its checks module by type, beside the `test_*` callables, and runs each as one more check: it searches it once a run (`searched(margin)`, which a check reading the same worst shares), prints the `Worst` row, and fails where the margin does not pass or its truth disagrees beyond `agree`. `test_derivation.py` collects the same checks, and `report_results.py` prints the margin table, with each load case's worst, from `discover_margins()`. The CAD part template's `run_all.py` carries the same code; without the engine it finds no margins.
  - **The holding margin**, `verification.HOLDING`, is one margin over `Coordinates` within `WORKSPACE`, a `Choice` of the load-case table's rows, a `Direction("xy")` read by a case that names a plane, and every band in `params.BANDS`. Its truth is the truth model's own inverse dynamics with the case's force at the tool, then held for 500 steps under that force, the settle the old `test_truth_worst_case_torque` ran. On the shipped arm it reads 1.1709 N·m, at the elbow, under the 15 N contact case pressing straight down, at m2's upper band end, in 3,166 evaluations and about two seconds. `worst_tip_force`'s exact norm stays, as a check that the search agrees with it, to 9e-16 here. A check holds the margin's `case` axis to the whole table, and one watches a flipped tool force disagree with the truth.
  - **σ_min** is `kinematics.SIGMA`, a margin over `Coordinates` within `WORKSPACE` with slack σ_min − `SIGMA_MIN`, confirmed against the truth model's site Jacobian. It reads 0.0020989 m/rad at the elbow-up edge, elbow = 0.30, where the 400 draws reported 0.0376 rather than the 0.0371 there.
  - **`params.py`** gains `BANDS = {name: (lo, hi)}`, with m2 ± 10 % shipped, and `params_checks.py` fails a band that names no parameter or leaves its nominal outside. `PARAMS` gains each motor's derated torque, `tau1` and `tau2`, which the description's actuators carry as `torque_limit`, so the margin reads its limit from the description and a settle clamps there. `mechanism.numeric` keeps each banded parameter as a keyword at its nominal, `g_num(*q, m2=0.88)`, so the derivation is evaluated at the band value the truth model was compiled with; `bands()` and `band_values(p)` spread `BANDS` into a space and back out of a point.
  - **The truth gate covers margins.** `layout.py` reads every module-level `Margin(...)` in a directory with a `mechanism.py`, and the full run fails on one with no `truth=` in a module with no `TRUTH_EXEMPT`; the fixture test watches it fail and clear. Both `layout.py` copies stay identical outside their docstrings.
  - **Engine: `Worst.truth`.** A margin with `truth=` is confirmed by `search` itself, at the worst point and the pre-polish best, the very `Point`s `fn` read, since `Worst.at` is a plain dict and a state cannot be rebuilt from it in general. `Worst.truth` is a `Confirmed(value, pre_polish, gap, agree, agrees)`, None without a truth, and the row prints it. A disagreement, or a nan, is recorded rather than raised; a truth that raises `Infeasible` at a point `fn` evaluated raises, naming the point. Its evaluations are not counted in `n`.
- **The docs say it once each** ([#82](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/82)).
  - **The derivation standards.** *Evidence over an envelope* replaces the three-tier method rule (an exact set method, else a multi-start optimiser, else a dense sweep with its step) with one: declare a `Margin` over every axis the set has, and the search is `armature.search`. Every tolerance granted is a band on the margins it moves, so the corner sweep is the search's band corners rather than a run of its own. σ_min is bounded by a margin. The section also says how a note cites the `Worst` row: the value and each case's worst rather than `at` alone, since mirror-image ties swap with the seed; `at` in the units `fn` reads, radians for a hinge; and coverage as a volume fraction, good to about ±1–2 points at the default budget, pooled over every `Choice` combination, so a per-case coverage is a search per case. Constants that are not searched reach `fn` by closure.
  - **`armature-derive`** points its σ_min margin, its worst-case torque, its tolerance corners and its holding truth check at the standards and at `HOLDING`.
  - **The review checklist** reads a margin's `Worst` row: the space against the requirement, coverage, the pre-polish best against the value, the truth's agreement, and a re-run at the reviewer's own seed. Its σ_min probe asks for the margin, and its load-case probe for the `Choice` and `Direction` axes. The red-team agent's *Worst case* step points at it.
  - **The spec template's §9** states each check's worst case over the envelope, or names the posture, direction, sign and band value its hand arithmetic assumed.
  - **`references/model-layout.md`** lists the search in the engine, and its truth gate covers margins. `armature-cad` hands its tolerance grants to derive as bands.
  - **Glossary.** `CONTEXT.md` gains **Margin**, **Search space**, **Band** and **Coverage**, and **Corner sweep** becomes the corners of a search's band axes.
  - **Engine.** `Worst`'s docstring says coverage is pooled over every combination, and that a `Case` carries none.

### The derivation gets a truth model

Every `armature-derive` milestone is checked against an independent MuJoCo model of the mechanism, built from the same parameter block. The reviews on the first real project found derivations whose arithmetic was right and whose model was wrong — a frozen degree of freedom, a textbook formula outside its assumptions — and the checkpoint's self-consistency checks could not see any of it, because the assumption sat on both sides. [#48](https://github.com/rioharper/armature/issues/48) tracks the effort.

- `scripts/env-probe.sh` checks **MuJoCo** in the core tier, beside SymPy, SciPy and NumPy, with the same `absent` / `error: <Type>` / version reading. Core rather than a tier because a machine that cannot import it cannot pass a derivation checkpoint at all. `pinocchio` joins the named checks as an optional second backend for chain dynamics — never core, since it has no Windows pip wheel.
- `references/environment-record.md` documents both; `init` and `armature-plan` name a missing MuJoCo as Phase 0 work that the analysis phase waits on, not a footnote.
- The model template gains **`truth/`**, the truth model: a MuJoCo model of the mechanism generated from `params.py`, sorted first in the run, with a chain builder that reads the DH table, link COMs, masses, inertias and the gravity vector, and a seeded sampler every other milestone's checks draw from. It imports the parameter block and nothing else from the model — a truth model that borrows the derivation's frames carries the derivation's assumptions on both sides of every comparison.
- **Milestone 1's checkpoint now checks FK and the Jacobian against it** at sampled configurations, printing the sample size and seed. The finite-difference check stays; it was never a substitute, since both its sides carry the same length function.
- `params.py` gains **`Q_LIMITS`, the envelope** — the sampler draws from it and the simulator model carries it as its joint ranges — and the shipped 2R arm's links gain a physical inertia tensor on every axis. `diag(0, 0, Izz)` gives a planar derivation the same M, C and g, but that zero is the planar assumption written into the parameter block, and a rigid body cannot be built from it.
- **A mode is checked against a linearisation of the truth model.** `truth/modes.py` returns M(q), K = ∂(bias force)/∂q by central differences of the simulator, and the frequencies solving K v = ω² M v — **signed**, so a divergent configuration reads negative instead of producing a frequency that looks fine. `dynamics/lagrangian.py` gains the analytic K = ∂g/∂q and `small_oscillation_modes`; Milestone 2 checks the 2R arm's hanging modes (0.813 and 1.845 Hz) against the linearisation to 7e-11 Hz, and checks that K has the rank the note claims. A mode is an eigenvalue of (K, M) and a diagonal entry of K is not one unless both matrices are diagonal — that mistake produces a plausible, dimensionally correct number about a motion the mechanism cannot make, and it is a finding on the first project the plugin ran.
- The review checklist's physics family gains the probe: a mode named with no linearisation behind it is a Major, and one sizing a controller bandwidth or a structural member is a Blocker.
- **Every equilibrium claim is checked by a settle.** `truth/equilibrium.py` releases the model at a posture under held commands and an optional wrench and reports where it came to rest, the constraint forces there, and whether it came to rest at all. Energy leaves by **quench** — velocity zeroed whenever the model turns back on itself — rather than by damping, which would put a constant into the model the parameter block never claimed and which would then be in every dynamics check too; it also has no tuning, where an overdamped mechanism relaxes at k/c and can take minutes of simulated time. The statics checks module holds g(q) at sampled postures for five hundred steps and asserts the model is still there, and asserts that a 20 % wrong shoulder torque is not: an algebraic balance is silent about every coordinate the derivation froze, so "balanced at this instant" and "still there" differ exactly where a frozen degree of freedom is free.
- **A body on cables, legs or wheels gets the second builder.** `truth/floating.py` builds the plan glossary's other convention family — a floating base plus joints — from `FLOATING_BODIES` and `FLOATING_CABLES` in the parameter block: a free joint carrying the body's inertia, any links hung off it by their joints, and one spatial tendon per cable. **A cable is unilateral**: the tendon carries a rest-length deadband from zero to its unstretched length and a stiffness of EA/L, so it pulls when stretched and does nothing at all when slack, and `truth.cable_forces` reads a slack cable as exactly zero rather than as a small number that survives a tolerance — and reads a *negative* force, a cable pushing, rather than clamping it away, so a deadband that never reached the model fails the check instead of guaranteeing it. That is what a bilateral tension solve cannot see — a pose it calls feasible, every cable in tension, can be one where a cable hangs slack and the load it was assumed to carry goes somewhere the derivation never looked. The truth package's own fixture hangs a body on three cables and settles it to the hand-computed pose (m g / 2 on each of the taut pair, 0 N on the third, to 1e-6 m). Both builders name their entry point `build`, so a project deletes the one its machine is not and `truth.build` is the one that remains; the sampler is the exception and says so, since a floating body's pose is not a box in `Q_LIMITS`. `references/model-layout.md` and the derive skill's Milestone 0 name both builders and which convention family each serves.
- **Evidence over an envelope is a seeded sample, and every inverted matrix is swept.** The derivation standards gain the section: a claim about a set is evidenced by a seeded random sample of that set with its size and seed stated, drawn from the truth package's one sampler, and a **named configuration is never the evidence** — home, q = 0, outstretched, a workspace corner are the mechanism's symmetry planes, which is where a wrong assumption cancels. Named configurations move to `report_results.py`, for the reader, printed beside the sweep minima. Every checks module in the template now draws from `truth.sample` rather than a generator of its own, so "over the envelope" is one set of configurations across the model. `params.py` gains **`WORKSPACE`** beside `Q_LIMITS` — the envelope is what the joints can reach, the intended workspace is what the requirements name, and a singularity is allowed to live in the gap — with `SIGMA_MIN`, the bound the derivation needs there, and `truth.sample_workspace` draws from it. Milestone 1 gains the mandatory **singular-value sweep**: `kinematics.singular_values` returns σ(J_v) per configuration, the checks module asserts σ_min ≥ 0.035 m/rad over 400 sampled workspace configurations and prints that over the full envelope it falls to 0.0009 at the elbow singularity, which is the machine rather than a failure. σ_min is a rate, not a tolerance: its reciprocal is the joint speed and joint torque one unit of Cartesian motion costs at the worst posture.
- **A closed form from a book is checked against the truth model before the note cites it.** The derivation standards' book-data rule extends to formulas and to algorithms: a closed form is compared against the truth model at *sampled* configurations — never at the tidy posture where the cosines are all one, which is where a formula outside its class most often happens to be right — with the assumption it rests on named as a module constant the failure message quotes; a reference algorithm is tagged `standard` and cited by book and chapter in `00_setup.md`, since reading a derivation against its book is the red team's job on one and it needs the book named. The shipped arm's two closed forms become the pattern: `moment_arm_torques` generalises off q = 0 and is checked against the truth model's gravity torque over 24 sampled postures, naming `MOMENT_ARM_ASSUMES`; the small-oscillation mode check names `MODES_ASSUME`. A formula is the cheapest wrong number a derivation can produce — its arithmetic is somebody else's and it reproduces — and the first project through this plugin sized a motor off a bifilar yaw stiffness on converging cables, 27× low.
- The review checklist's physics family gains the probe: a closed form with no applicability check is a Major and a Blocker where it sizes a part; an algorithm cited without its chapter is a Minor.
- **The plan puts the truth model before the first analytic milestone.** `armature-plan`'s analysis-phase arc now reads: parameter block and envelope, then *generate the truth model from it*, then the kinematic model and the sizing — the truth-model leaf carrying `armature-derive` as Executor. A derivation with nothing to be checked against on the day it is written gets its wrong assumption found at review months later instead of at its own checkpoint. Later simulation tasks — a bench model, a rig model for controller work, a prototype's kill criterion — **consume `analysis/model/truth/`** rather than starting from nothing.
- **The red team recomputes rather than reading a reported pass.** The agent's inputs list names the truth package among what a derivation review receives (it is not a milestone's counterpart, so it is easy to leave out of a partition, and without it no truth check can be re-run); its *Do the check* step says to re-run the truth checks **at its own seed**, and to read the sampled configurations `report_truth.py` prints — a pass over configurations that all sit on the symmetry planes is a pass nobody earned. It also challenges every `TRUTH_EXEMPT` the way it already challenges every `LAYOUT_EXEMPT`.
- The review checklist's physics family now **opens with the five error families by name** — frozen degree of freedom, worst case at named configurations, load-case sign, formula outside its assumptions, unswept singular values — as a table pointing each at the probe that hunts it and the one file that owns its rule. No new family: all five live inside physics-and-math, and all five were found on the first real project through this plugin in one review, after its checkpoint had passed.
- **Pinocchio is wired in as the optional second backend it was probed for.** `truth/inverse.py` gains `available()`, `resolve()`, `pinocchio_chain()` and a `backend` argument: where Pinocchio imports it builds the same serial chain from the same parameter block — same joint order, same placements, same inertias, same gravity, read through `truth.chain`'s own DH reduction — and runs its own RNEA, and where it does not the call falls back to MuJoCo and prints the fallback rather than passing quietly under a backend name that never ran. Milestone 2's truth check runs against every available backend and says which. It is never mandatory: no Windows pip wheel, no unilateral constraints, and the environment record's `Pinocchio` line reading `absent` costs a project nothing where `MUJOCO=absent` is a blocker. `truth/chain.py`'s float reader becomes public as `as_float`, since a second backend must read every constant through the same substitution.
- **The truth model cannot be silently skipped: `layout.py` gains the truth gate.** Every checks module outside the truth package defines a `test_truth_*` callable or carries `TRUTH_EXEMPT = "<why>"`, and the full run fails on one with neither. The prefix is a naming convention rather than a registry because `layout.py` imports nothing but the standard library — it reads function names with `ast`, so it runs on the machine where MuJoCo will not import, which is exactly the machine most likely to have skipped the truth model. The size table gains a truth column (a count, `n/a`, or the exemption), the exemption reader is generalised to take the constant's name so both gates share one parser, and a fixture test in a temporary directory watches the gate fail on a bare checks module, pass on an argued exemption, refuse `TRUTH_EXEMPT = True` and `""`, and stand down where there is no truth package. That last is what lets the same file ship unchanged in **armature-cad**'s part template, where it reports n/a and passes. The template's truth checks are renamed to the convention (`test_truth_fk_and_jacobian`, `test_truth_inverse_dynamics`, `test_truth_modes`, `test_truth_held_posture_stays`, `test_truth_moment_arm_form`, `test_truth_worst_case_torque`). `truth/chain_checks.py` gains one more — every sampled workspace pose sits clear of the joint limits the builder gave the model, since on a limit every downstream truth check reads the end-stop's force rather than the mechanism's — and `params_checks.py` carries the template's one worked **`TRUTH_EXEMPT`**: a block of data has no claim a simulator can be asked about, its constants being held against the truth model where they are used. `references/model-layout.md` states the ordering rule by its reason — the truth package runs before every module that makes a truth *claim*, and the parameter block, which makes none, may precede it.
- **An assumption that freezes a coordinate is tested by releasing it.** `truth/release.py` settles the truth model with the analytic command held on every coordinate the derivation kept and **nothing at all** on the one the assumption fixed, and reports the signed displacement — releasing is the absence of a command, because the derivation named nothing that holds the coordinate there. `00_setup.md`'s numbered assumptions become a table with a **Released?** column carrying either the check's name and what moved, or one sentence on why the coordinate is not in the parameter block and what adding it would take; the derive skill's Step 0 states the rule, the standards say what goes in the cell, and the checklist probe reads it — a blank cell is a Major, and a coordinate that releases past the margin is a Blocker.
- **The check has teeth, and the template shows it failing.** `truth/release_checks.py` carries a degenerate fixture: a locked-elbow "derivation" of the shipped 2R arm whose shoulder torque is right to the digit for the machine it describes, whose moment-arm and finite-difference self-checks all pass, and which — released in a model that has the elbow — swings 1.57 rad and takes the shoulder with it. Wrapped as an expected failure, so `run_all.py` and `pytest` stay green while the failure stays printed: a check nobody has watched fail is a check with no evidence behind it.
- **"Worst", "any" and "anywhere" are computed, never sampled.** The derivation standards gain the rule: an exact set method where the set is convex, a multi-start optimiser where none exists, a dense sweep only with its grid step printed beside the result. `verification.py`'s search is rebuilt on `grid_search` and `optimiser_search` and returns the worse of the two with the step, because a grid cannot see a peak narrower than its step and an optimiser cannot certify a global maximum — on the shipped arm the optimiser beats the 25-point grid at every joint, by up to 0.2 %. `params.py` gains **`LOAD_CASES`**, a table of `(class, magnitude, direction set, sign)` rows with **both signs of every signed class**, and a new `params_checks.py` fails a signed class carrying one sign: the two braking rows differ by 4 % at the shoulder, so the one-sign reading that blocked the first project through this plugin undersizes the actuator. A row whose direction is a *set* rather than a direction (`PLANE_XY`) is answered exactly — the worst tip force over a circle of headings is the norm of the Jacobian column projected onto its plane, not thirty-six headings tried.
- **Milestone 3 gains its mandatory truth check**, and it is a *settle* rather than a recomputation. The worst-case torque is held at the posture the search found while the load case is applied to the truth model as a real force — `truth.site_wrench` converts a force at a site into the body wrench `settle` takes, at the body's centre of mass, which is where MuJoCo's `xfrc_applied` acts and not its frame origin. The shipped arm stays put to 4e-13 rad. Recomputing τ = g − J_vᵀ f from the truth model's own gravity torque and site Jacobian would have been the easy check and is not enough: the mapping sits on both sides, so a flipped force sign is flipped in the comparison too — this plugin's own failure mode, rebuilt one level up. `params.py` becomes a discovered milestone module of its own, so a load-case table that has lost a sign fails before any milestone that sizes against it runs.
- The review checklist's physics family gains two more probes: a worst case taken at named configurations or by a handful of samples, and a signed load case read in one sign — each a Blocker where a part is bought against it.
- The review checklist's physics family gains two probes: a claim whose configurations are all named — a Major, a Blocker when those postures are the symmetry planes and the requirement says "anywhere" — and an inverted matrix with no singular-value sweep, where a rank claimed and never computed, or a singularity inside the intended workspace, is a Blocker.
- The derivation standards gain the register rule for a truth-checked result: say whether the truth model **confirmed or surprised** the prediction, with the sample size and seed. A truth check reported as "passed" with no number is claimed, not shown.
- **Milestone 2's dynamics are checked against the truth model's inverse dynamics** at sampled states — `truth/inverse.py`, MuJoCo's own recursive algorithm, which never saw T or V. τ = M q̈ + C q̇ + g agrees to 4e-15 N m on the shipped 2R arm, and a halved Coriolis matrix or a flipped gravity sign fails it. `params.py` gains the rate envelope (`QD_LIMITS`, `QDD_LIMITS`) and the sampler gains `sample_states`, because at rest C multiplies zero and a posture-only dynamics check is a statics check wearing a dynamics name.
- The review checklist's physics family gains the probe: a milestone that claims a torque with nothing but self-consistency behind it is a Major, and one that sizes an actuator from it is a Blocker.
- `references/model-layout.md` gains **The truth package** (a milestone package and a library package at once, first in the run, importing the parameter block only); the derive skill's Milestone 0 builds it and Milestone 1 names its mandatory check; `CONTEXT.md` gains **Truth model**, avoiding digital twin, sim and reference model.
- The model template's **Lagrangian is built by `sympy.physics.mechanics`** — frames off the DH table, points given velocities by the one- and two-point theorems, a `RigidBody` per link, `LagrangesMethod` for the equations. The angular velocity read out of Ṙ Rᵀ and the hand Euler-Lagrange loop are gone, so an ω sign or a dropped Coriolis term is impossible by construction rather than caught later. M, C, g and V are unchanged to 1e-12 on the shipped 2R arm, now pinned by a self-test; `dynamics/` no longer imports `kinematics.py`, sharing only the parameter block. The derive skill's Milestone 2 calls symmetry, skew-symmetry and energy conservation **self-consistency** checks, which is all they ever were.
